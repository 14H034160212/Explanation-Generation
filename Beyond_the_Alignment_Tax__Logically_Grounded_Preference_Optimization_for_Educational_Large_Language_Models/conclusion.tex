\section{Conclusion}

We presented \textbf{RLearner-LLM}, a cross-domain alignment framework that addresses the root cause of logical misalignment in DPO-trained educational models: the reward signal blindspot that causes standard preference optimization to reinforce verbosity over logical correctness. By fusing NLI entailment probability with a verifier fluency score into a composite Hybrid-DPO reward, we automatically construct preference pairs that guide the model toward explanations that are simultaneously logically grounded and linguistically natural---overcoming the alignment tax that afflicts single-signal optimization.

Our experiments across five academic domains and two model architectures yield three concrete findings: (1) the hybrid reward consistently improves both logical entailment (up to 6$\times$ over SFT) and answer coverage (up to +29 pp ACR) without requiring human annotation; (2) the marginal benefit of Hybrid-DPO shifts from NLI gains to ACR gains as base model quality increases, confirming the framework's robustness across the model capability spectrum; and (3) verbosity bias in LLM-as-a-judge evaluation is a quantifiable and overcomeable failure mode, addressed by aligning models to produce logically dense rather than stylistically elaborate outputs.

These findings suggest that the framework is generalizable beyond educational NLG to any task where outputs have a verifiable logical structure---including medical reasoning, mathematical explanation, and code generation. Future work will explore dynamic weighting of the hybrid reward components across domains, and the application of this preference construction paradigm to larger-scale online RLHF pipelines.
