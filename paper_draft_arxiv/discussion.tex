\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why the Hybrid Signal Works}
NLI verifies that $E$ implies the correct option; the verifier captures clarity invisible to NLI. Each alone degenerates (answer-repeating snippets or verbosity bias), so the joint reward (Eq.~\ref{eq:hybrid_reward}) filters both extremes and steers the policy to the alignment frontier's upper-right (Fig.~\ref{fig:alignment_tax}). The chosen/rejected NLI gap of $17$--$18\times$ alongside indistinguishable verifier scores replicates across all three architectures, confirming NLI as the discriminating signal and the verifier as a fluency regulariser. \textbf{Robustness to question-quality filtering.}\label{sec:tier_b_ablation} A stricter Tier-B Cardiff filter (\texttt{deleted}{=}0, $\geq{10}\%$ 5-star endorsement) shrinks the pool $7\times$ yet \emph{lifts} Hybrid-DPO NLI by $+48\%$ and ACR by $+13.3$\,pp (Appendix~\ref{sec:tierb_appendix}). Stacking a Tier-C \emph{strict-discriminator} filter (drop questions whose author key differs from the modal student answer) preserves the pattern: across all six architecture--corpus cells every cell improves ACR and four improve NLI, with the largest lifts on LLaMA-2-13B ($+219\%$/$+204\%$), while Qwen3-8B stays ACR-up/NLI-flat (Appendix~\ref{sec:tierc_appendix}).

\subsection{Architecture Effects and Entailment Headroom}
\label{sec:arch-effects}
Hybrid-DPO's marginal NLI gain is inversely related to the SFT NLI baseline: across all $15$ (architecture, domain) cells the relative NLI gain correlates with the SFT NLI baseline at Pearson $r{=}{-}0.81$ ($p{=}3\times10^{-4}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:headroom}), so the benefit is set by \emph{entailment headroom}, not architecture or scale. LLaMA-2-13B (lowest SFT NLI, $0.05$--$0.27$) sees the largest lifts (peak $+0.339$/$4.9\times$); Qwen3-8B (higher SFT NLI) shifts gains onto ACR ($+8$ to $+29$\,pp); and Gemma~4 E4B-it (4.5B effective) improves NLI on $4$ of $5$ domains, matching the LLaMA-2 peak with $\sim$3$\times$ fewer parameters and being the first single-pass result to surpass ILearner-LLM (K=5) on Auckland Law. The lone non-improvement, Sydney Biology, has the highest SFT NLI in our study ($0.2469$). Since $H_M$'s ACR gate is a floor not a ceiling, a stronger ACR weight is a natural way to recover Qwen3's ACR profile. \textbf{Recency control (newer model).} Repeating the Cardiff pipeline on Llama-3-8B (newer and smaller than LLaMA-2-13B) lifts an already-high SFT baseline (NLI $0.305$, ACR $0.796$) to NLI $0.551$ ($+80.7\%$) and ACR $0.876$ ($+8.0$\,pp), so the SFT$\to$DPO improvement holds on a modern architecture, not only the older LLaMA-2-13B (NLI-only DPO here; the verifier was unavailable on that machine).

\subsection{Directly Optimising Reasoning Quality}
\label{sec:reasoning-reward}
Answer entailment (NLI) and coverage (ACR) are proxies: an explanation can entail the correct option by restating it, without giving a valid reason. We therefore test whether the policy can be trained to produce \emph{better reasoning}, not merely higher entailment, and whether any gain survives a length control, since verbose outputs inflate LLM-judge win rates.

\textbf{Reasoning-quality reward.} From the Cardiff Tier-C training prompts we sample three on-policy explanations per question from the Gemma~4 SFT model and rank them with a \emph{pairwise} reasoning judge (DeepSeek-R1-32B) asked which explanation better justifies \emph{why} the stated answer is correct, explicitly ignoring length and formatting, with order randomised to blunt position bias. This yields $321$ preference pairs. To remove a length confound at the source we keep the length-matched subset ($112$ pairs; chosen $46$ vs rejected $44$ words).

\textbf{Length-preserving optimisation.} Plain sequence-level DPO on this reward drifts the policy toward the base model's verbose register; we instead use SimPO, whose length-normalised, reference-free objective removes the incentive to lengthen. The resulting policy stays concise: $68$ words on the held-out test versus $66$ for SFT (plain DPO $60$; reward-filtered self-distillation $44$).

\textbf{Held-out evaluation.} On all $191$ Cardiff Tier-C test questions, and at matched length, three judges independent of the reward (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B, and the frontier gpt-5-mini) prefer the SimPO policy's reasoning to SFT's, by $70\%$, $72\%$, and $82\%$ of non-tied pairs ($p\ll10^{-3}$, sign test); a reward-family reference judge (DeepSeek-R1) agrees at $80\%$. Plain DPO and the reward-filtered self-distillation variant are weaker or mixed (Appendix~\ref{sec:reasoning_appendix}, Table~\ref{tab:reasoning_judges}). \textbf{Seed stability.} Retraining SimPO under three independent random seeds gives a Qwen2.5 non-tied win rate of $76.4\pm0.8\%$ (range $75.3$--$77.1\%$), so the advantage is a stable property of the method rather than the noise of a single run.

\textbf{Human study.} A blinded study with four independent raters on the $24$ length-matched pairs corroborates the automatic judges' direction: raters prefer the SimPO policy in $63\%$ of non-tied judgements ($56$ vs $33$; sign test $p\approx0.02$; per-item majority $13$ vs $5$). The margin is smaller than for the LLM judges and inter-rater agreement is modest ($49\%$), reflecting the subjectivity of fine-grained reasoning judgements (Appendix~\ref{sec:human_appendix}).

\textbf{Scope.} Two judges are reward-independent and every comparison is length-matched; larger human validation and extension beyond Gemma~4/Cardiff remain future work.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}
Four limitations apply. (i) LLaMA-2 on Auckland Law (NLI $0.3229$) is surpassed by ILearner-LLM (K=5) at $0.3996$; Gemma~4 closes the gap (\S\ref{sec:arch-effects}). (ii)~\textbf{Circular evaluation}: NLI uses the same \texttt{deberta-v3-small} that scored training pairs. An independent held-out scorer (DeBERTa-v3-large) agrees in $12/15$ main cells and $17/21$ overall (all five Gemma-4 domains), evidence against reward-hacking; it does not corroborate some LLaMA-2 gains, so we scope the headline claim to entailment/coverage (Appendix~\ref{sec:heldout_appendix}). (iii) The PeerWise SFT corpus is undergraduate-authored; partial justifications cap overlap metrics, motivating an expert-curated rerun. (iv)~\textbf{Scope: the benefit tracks entailment headroom} ($r{=}{-}0.81$, \S\ref{sec:arch-effects}): where SFT is already strong the gain shrinks or shifts to coverage (Qwen3-8B; Sydney), so Hybrid-DPO most helps weak or learnersourced generators, not ones whose SFT explanations are already answer-faithful.
