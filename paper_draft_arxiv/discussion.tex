\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why the Hybrid Signal Works}
NLI verifies that $E$ implies the correct option; the verifier captures clarity invisible to NLI. Each alone degenerates (answer-repeating snippets or verbosity bias), so the joint reward (Eq.~\ref{eq:hybrid_reward}) filters both extremes and steers the policy to the alignment frontier's upper-right (Fig.~\ref{fig:alignment_tax}). The chosen/rejected NLI gap of $17$--$18\times$ alongside indistinguishable verifier scores replicates across all three architectures, confirming NLI as the discriminating signal and the verifier as a fluency regulariser. \textbf{Tier-B robustness}: a stricter Cardiff filter (\texttt{deleted}{=}0, $5$-star endorsement share $\geq 0.10$) shrinks the pool $7\times$ ($7{,}309{\to}1{,}041$) yet \emph{lifts} Hybrid-DPO NLI by $+48\%$ and ACR by $+13.3$\,pp, with the chosen/rejected NLI gap widening to $18\times$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:tierb_appendix}, Table~\ref{tab:tierB_ablation})\label{sec:tier_b_ablation}. \textbf{Tier-C strict-discriminator}: using the full PeerWise answer-submission logs (now obtained for Cardiff and Sydney), we additionally drop every question whose author key disagrees with the \emph{modal student answer}, a conservative mis-key/ambiguity proxy that was infeasible at submission time for lack of per-option counts. Stacked on Tier-B, this strictest filter ($1{,}041{\to}955$ Cardiff, $459{\to}384$ Sydney) preserves the per-architecture pattern of the main results: across all six architecture--corpus cells \emph{every} cell improves ACR and four improve NLI. The NLI lifts are largest on LLaMA-2-13B ($+219\%$ Cardiff, $+204\%$ Sydney, from its lowest SFT baselines) and strong on Gemma 4 E4B-it ($+109\%$ / $+90\%$), while Qwen3-8B reproduces its default-filter ACR-up/NLI-flat behaviour (Appendix~\ref{sec:tierc_appendix}, Table~\ref{tab:tierC_ablation}).

\subsection{Architecture Effects and Entailment Headroom}
\label{sec:arch-effects}
Hybrid-DPO's marginal NLI gain is inversely related to the SFT NLI baseline. LLaMA-2-13B SFT spans $0.05$--$0.27$ across domains; RLearner-LLM lifts these to $0.32$--$0.43$ (peak $+0.339$/$4.9\times$ on UK Med Y1). Qwen3-8B SFT sits at $0.16$--$0.32$, so gains shift onto ACR ($+9$ to $+29$\,pp). Gemma 4 E4B-it (4.5B effective) improves NLI on $4$ of $5$ domains, $+0.229$ on UK Med Y2 ($2.4\times$) matches the LLaMA-2 peak with $\sim$3$\times$ fewer effective parameters, and is the first single-pass result to surpass ILearner-LLM (K=5) on Auckland Law. The non-improvement is Sydney Biology, where SFT NLI ($0.2469$) is already the highest in our study. These patterns suggest Hybrid-DPO tracks \emph{entailment headroom} rather than raw model scale. On three of the four Gemma 4 NLI-gain domains, NLI rises with a modest ACR reduction ($-10.3$ to $-1.3$\,pp on Cardiff, Med Y1, Med Y2; Auckland Law gains both, $+11.9\%$/$+3.9$\,pp); $H_M$'s ACR gate is a floor, not a ceiling, so a stronger ACR weight is a natural extension to recover Qwen3's ACR profile.

\subsection{Directly Optimising Reasoning Quality}
\label{sec:reasoning-reward}
Answer entailment (NLI) and coverage (ACR) are proxies: an explanation can entail the correct option by restating it, without giving a valid reason. We therefore test whether the policy can be trained to produce \emph{better reasoning}, not merely higher entailment, and whether any gain survives a length control, since verbose outputs inflate LLM-judge win rates.

\textbf{Reasoning-quality reward.} From the Cardiff Tier-C training prompts we sample three on-policy explanations per question from the Gemma~4 SFT model and rank them with a \emph{pairwise} reasoning judge (DeepSeek-R1-32B) asked which explanation better justifies \emph{why} the stated answer is correct, explicitly ignoring length, fluency, and formatting, with presentation order randomised to blunt position bias. This yields $321$ preference pairs. To remove a length confound at the source we keep the length-matched subset ($112$ pairs; chosen $46$ vs rejected $44$ words). An independent judge from a different family (Llama-3-8B) agrees with these preferences on $69\%$ of the length-matched pairs, indicating a cross-judge reasoning preference rather than one model's idiosyncrasy or a length artefact.

\textbf{Length-preserving optimisation.} Plain sequence-level DPO on this reward drifts the policy toward the base model's verbose register; we instead use SimPO, whose length-normalised, reference-free objective removes the incentive to lengthen. The resulting policy stays concise: $68$ words on the held-out test versus $66$ for SFT (plain DPO $60$; a reward-filtered on-policy self-distillation variant $44$).

\textbf{Held-out evaluation.} On all $191$ Cardiff Tier-C test questions, and at matched length, two judges that never touched the reward (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B) both prefer the SimPO policy's reasoning to SFT's, $70\%$ and $72\%$ of non-tied pairs ($130/55$ and $138/53$; $p\ll10^{-3}$, sign test), and a reward-family reference judge (DeepSeek-R1) agrees at $80\%$ ($124/31$). The weaker plain-DPO policy is preferred at $66\%$/$57\%$; the reward-filtered self-distillation variant is mixed ($62\%$/$51\%$). This is a length-controlled, cross-judge reasoning-quality improvement over SFT, obtained without changing the base model or inflating output length (Appendix~\ref{sec:reasoning_appendix}, Table~\ref{tab:reasoning_judges}). \textbf{Seed stability.} Retraining SimPO under three independent random seeds gives a Qwen2.5 non-tied win rate of $76.4\pm0.8\%$ (range $75.3$--$77.1\%$), so the advantage is a stable property of the method rather than the noise of a single run.

\textbf{Scope.} The judges are LLMs; two are independent of the reward and the comparison is length-matched, which addresses the two standard LLM-judge failure modes (reward circularity and verbosity bias), but a human step-validity study remains the gold standard and is the natural next step. The result is shown for Gemma~4 on Cardiff; extending it across architectures and corpora is future work.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}
Three limitations apply. (i) LLaMA-2 on Auckland Law (NLI $0.3229$) is surpassed by ILearner-LLM (K=5) at $0.3996$ (Gemma 4 closes the gap, \S\ref{sec:arch-effects}); adding targeted iteration is future work. (ii)~\textbf{Circular-evaluation risk}: NLI is reported using the same \texttt{deberta-v3-small} that scores training pairs. Three properties mitigate this: dual-signal coupling, $H_M$'s independent ACR gate, and out-of-family GPT-4o-mini pairwise judgements (Table~\ref{tab:pairwise_1}); we now also test it directly with an independent held-out scorer: DeBERTa-v3-large (never used in training) agrees with the training scorer on the SFT$\to$DPO improvement direction in $12/15$ main cells and $17/21$ overall, confirming all five Gemma-4 domains and every non-improvement cell, which is inconsistent with pure reward-hacking. The held-out scorer does not corroborate some LLaMA-2 gains (Auckland Law, UK Med Y1), scoping our headline claim to answer entailment/coverage rather than full reasoning soundness (Appendix~\ref{sec:heldout_appendix}, Table~\ref{tab:heldout_nli}); we take a first, length-controlled step toward reasoning soundness in \S\ref{sec:reasoning-reward}. (iii) The PeerWise SFT corpus is undergraduate-authored; partial justifications cap overlap-style metrics, motivating an expert-curated rerun.
