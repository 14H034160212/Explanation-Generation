\textbf{Shared pool, entailment metrics.} With every variant trained on pairs from the same candidates, NLI alone is the strongest entailment reward: held-out NLI rises from 0.307 to 0.473 and masked held-out NLI from 0.062 to 0.133 (95\% CI $[+0.047, +0.095]$), at unchanged length (53 vs.\ 55 words) and with fewer answer copies (47\% vs.\ 52\%). Adding the verifier only dilutes this. The $H_A$ gain grows with the NLI weight, and verifier-only DPO \emph{lowers} training NLI (0.261$\to$0.160) while nearly doubling the length (99 words), the length bias that motivated a label-free reward in the first place. $H_M$, the reward behind Table~\ref{tab:main}, lies in between and is stable over three seeds; keeping only the 117 pairs where both signals agree removes the gain. A reward computed on the \emph{masked} explanation (93 pairs) does no better than NLI on 93 random pairs, so at this pool size the gain follows the number of pairs, not the masking.

\textbf{Shared pool, reasoning judges.} Higher entailment is not what reasoning judges reward. Three judges (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-4-31B; 500 items, order-flipped) prefer SFT to both the NLI and the $H_M$ policies (38--42\% non-tie wins, $p{<}10^{-3}$ each). Lighter NLI training (1 or 2 epochs, or $\beta{=}0.5$) restores parity but gives up most of the entailment gain. We therefore add a reasoning-judge score (Qwen3-8B, never used for evaluation) to the pool and train with SimPO. A first version won 60--61\% against SFT under two judges, but its chosen candidates were 8 words longer than the rejected ones, and on items where the two outputs are within 10 words of each other the advantage disappears. Restricting pairs to candidates within 15 words of each other, the rule of \S\ref{sec:reasoning-reward}, removes this: the judge-only policy writes as much as SFT (54 vs.\ 55 words) and, pooled over three seeds, is preferred by all three judges (52\%, 56\%, 54\%; $p{=}0.10$, $8{\times}10^{-7}$, $0.01$), a small but consistent gain without an NLI change. Summing the judge and NLI scores on the same length-matched pairs gives the policy we would recommend: masked held-out NLI still rises significantly (0.062$\to$0.096, CI $[+0.011, +0.057]$; held-out 0.307$\to$0.364) at the same length, reasoning judges rate it level with SFT (46\%, 52\%, 50\%), and all three prefer it to NLI-DPO (59\%, 59\%, 60\%; $p{<}3{\times}10^{-4}$ each). The two signals are complementary: NLI supplies a verifiable entailment gain, the reasoning judge keeps that gain from costing reasoning quality (Appendix~\ref{sec:g4shared_appendix}).}

\section{Gemma~4 Shared-Pool Ablation: Details}
\label{sec:g4shared_appendix}

\textbf{Pool and pairs.} 500 training prompts (100 per course) $\times$ 3 candidates sampled from the Gemma~4 SFT model at $T{=}0.7$. Every candidate is scored once by the training NLI scorer, the masked NLI score, the reproduced verifier (Appendix~\ref{sec:verifier_repro}) and the reasoning judge. For each variant the pair is the best- vs.\ worst-scoring candidate, kept if the score gap is at least 0.05 (0.25 on the 0--1 judge score). DPO: 5 epochs, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$, $\beta{=}0.1$, gradient accumulation 8, seed 42; seeds 2 and 3 for $H_M$, masked NLI and the 93-pair NLI control. SimPO: a new LoRA ($r{=}16$) on the merged SFT model, $\beta{=}2$, $\gamma{=}1$, 3 epochs, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-5}$.

\textbf{Reasoning-judge reward.} Qwen3-8B (thinking disabled) compares the three candidates of a prompt in all three pairs, each in both orders, with the evaluation judges' prompt; a win counts 1 and a tie 0.5, giving 0--4 wins per candidate. The judge reward is wins$/4$ (293 pairs); the summed reward is $0.5\cdot$wins$/4 + 0.5\cdot$NLI (390 pairs). Judge-chosen candidates are 8 words longer than rejected ones on average (63 vs.\ 55); the summed reward's are not (57 vs.\ 56) and have far higher NLI (0.50 vs.\ 0.09). Round 3 considers only candidate pairs within 15 words of each other and takes the one with the largest score gap: 182 judge pairs (chosen 43.7 vs.\ rejected 43.6 words) and 254 judge+NLI pairs (41.7 vs.\ 42.4 words; NLI 0.47 vs.\ 0.14).

\textbf{Judges, all comparisons.} Masked NLI vs.\ NLI: 57\% / 54\% / 57\% (Qwen2.5 / Llama-3.1 / Gemma-4-31B), i.e.\ the masked reward's outputs are judged better than NLI-DPO's although their entailment is lower. Masked NLI vs.\ 93 random NLI pairs: 48\% / 45\% / 46\%. Judge+NLI vs.\ judge-only SimPO: 40\% / 34\% / 34\%. NLI-only SimPO shortens outputs to 29 words and is judged worst (39\% / 36\% / 31\% vs.\ SFT).

\textbf{Length control.} LLM judges favour longer outputs (\S\ref{sec:verbosity}). Restricting each comparison to items where the two outputs differ by at most 10 words gives: round-2 judge-only SimPO vs.\ SFT 43\% / 53\% / 56\% (none significant, against 48\% / 60\% / 61\% on all items); length-matched judge-only SimPO (three seeds pooled) 51\% / 53\% / 52\%; length-matched judge+NLI vs.\ SFT 47\% / 52\% / 55\%, and vs.\ NLI-DPO 56\% / 59\% / 55\% (Llama $p{=}0.02$). NLI-DPO's deficit against SFT is also mostly length: on the same subset it is 44\% / 47\% / 49\%, and where NLI-DPO is the shorter output it wins only 30--40\%. The length-matched pair rule therefore matters for any judge-based comparison, and the gains of the round-3 policies are small; we report them as such.

\section{Reproducing the Verifier}
\label{sec:verifier_repro}

The Alpaca-7B verifier of \citet{bao2025exploring} used for Table~\ref{tab:main} (fully fine-tuned for 20 epochs) is no longer available. For the shared-pool ablation we retrained it from the same data (29,484 question-rating examples, same prompt) as a LoRA ($r{=}64$, all linear layers) on LLaMA-7B for 2 epochs, holding out 1,000 examples. On these it predicts the rating with mean absolute error 0.214 (0.243 for always predicting the mean; Pearson $r{=}0.45$). The labels are concentrated: 86\% round to 3. On the 328 Gemma~4 texts that still carry the original verifier's scores, old and new scores correlate at Pearson 0.73 (Spearman 0.69), with means 3.05 vs.\ 2.92. The reproduction keeps the property that matters for our argument: in the $H_M$ pairs of the shared pool the chosen explanation has the lower verifier score in 35\% of pairs, against 34\% with the original verifier on the merged pool (Table~\ref{tab:pair_stats}). All verifier numbers in Table~\ref{tab:g4shared} use the reproduction; all other tables use the original.
