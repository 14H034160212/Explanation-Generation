\begin{abstract}
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is an efficient alternative to PPO-based RLHF, but on knowledge-intensive tasks its preference signals---from human annotators or LLM judges---exhibit a \textit{verbosity bias} that rewards fluency over logical correctness. This reward-signal blindspot leaves a large logical gap: SFT models reach NLI entailment scores of only 0.05--0.27 despite fluent, confident-sounding text. We propose \textbf{RLearner-LLM} and its \textbf{Hybrid-DPO}: an automated preference-construction pipeline that fuses a DeBERTa-v3 NLI signal with a verifier LLM score, needing no human annotation while avoiding the ``alignment tax'' of single-signal optimization. Evaluated across five academic domains (Biology, Medicine, Law) with three base architectures (LLaMA-2-13B, Qwen3-8B, and Gemma 4 E4B-it), RLearner-LLM achieves up to 6.6$\times$ NLI improvement over SFT baselines, with NLI gains in $11$ of $15$ (architecture, domain) cells and consistent answer-coverage gains on stronger base models. On the 4.5B-effective Gemma 4 E4B-it, Hybrid-DPO lifts NLI in four of five domains (up to +143\%; the sole exception, Sydney Biology, already has a high SFT NLI), with faster inference across all five. The gains survive a \textit{strict-discriminator} question-quality filter (keeping only questions whose author key matches the modal student answer): answer-coverage improves in every architecture--corpus cell and NLI in four of six. An independent held-out NLI scorer never used in training reproduces the SFT$\to$DPO improvement direction in $12$ of $15$ main cells. A length-controlled follow-up then targets reasoning quality directly: using a pairwise reasoning-quality reward with a length-normalised objective, a compact model's explanations are preferred over its SFT baseline by three independent judges, including the frontier gpt-5-mini ($70$--$82\%$), at matched output length. Our Qwen3-8B RLearner-LLM wins $95\%$ of blind pairwise comparisons against its own SFT baseline. On the same judge, GPT-4o-mini's longer outputs in turn win $95\%$ against our concise output, and that judge also awards a verbose SFT baseline a $69\%$ win over our DPO-aligned model. These two reversals reproduce the verbosity bias on a frontier comparator, and argue for logic-aware automatic metrics (NLI, ACR) over LLM-as-a-judge on knowledge-intensive generation.\footnote{Code, training scripts, and per-domain evaluation artefacts (anonymised for review): \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Explanation-Generation-468D/}}
\end{abstract}
