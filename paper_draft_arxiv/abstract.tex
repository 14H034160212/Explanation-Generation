\begin{abstract}
Preference optimisation for educational explanations needs preference labels, and human or LLM-judge labels tend to favour longer answers. We study \textbf{RLearner-LLM}, a label-free alternative. For each multiple-choice question we sample several explanations from a fine-tuned model and score each with a natural-language-inference (NLI) model: the explanation is the premise, the correct option the hypothesis, and the score is the probability of entailment. We then train with DPO on the highest- versus lowest-scoring pair, optionally combining the NLI score with a verifier score. We evaluate on five PeerWise course datasets (biology, law, medicine) with three base models, using three checks: SFT and DPO scored in the same evaluation run, an NLI scorer never used in training, and an \emph{answer-masking test} that removes the correct answer's words from the explanation before scoring. The checks separate two outcomes. On LLaMA-2-13B, entailment rises up to five-fold, but the gain disappears under masking: the model has learned to restate the answer, and verifier-only DPO produces the same gain. On Gemma-4-E4B, entailment rises on four of five datasets under the held-out scorer and on all five under masking, while outputs get shorter and do not copy the answer more often. When every reward is trained on the same candidate pool, NLI alone gives the largest entailment gain and adding the verifier only dilutes it. Finally, we replace the NLI score with a pairwise reasoning judge and train with the length-normalised SimPO objective. At matched length, three independent LLM judges prefer the resulting explanations to SFT (70--84\% of non-tied pairs), as do four human raters (63\%). Those judges barely prefer the NLI-trained policy to SFT (51--58\%), and on all five Gemma~4 courses three judges prefer SFT to it (39--42\%). Adding the reasoning-judge score to the NLI score on length-matched pairs keeps a significant masked entailment gain, is judged level with SFT, and is preferred to the NLI-only policy by all three judges (59--60\%). Higher entailment and better-judged reasoning are therefore different targets, and the masking test is a cheap way to tell an entailment gain from answer restatement.\footnote{Code, preference data, adapters and every evaluation file (anonymised for review): \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Explanation-Generation-468D/}}
\end{abstract}
