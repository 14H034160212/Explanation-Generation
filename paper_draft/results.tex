\section{Experimental Setup}

We conduct our experiments using two distinct model architectures: LLaMA-2-13B and Qwen3-8B. All training is performed on a cluster of NVIDIA A100 GPUs using the TRL and PEFT libraries.

\subsection{Datasets and Preprocessing}
The evaluation spans five academic domains: Cardiff Biology, Sydney Biology, Auckland Law, and UK Medicine (Years 1 and 2). For each domain, we utilize a 100-question held-out test set. Input contexts are formatted as multi-choice questions with associated correct options.

\subsection{Training Configurations}
We employ a three-phase training pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)}: Models are tuned on 13,211 expert explanations for 3 epochs. We use LoRA with a rank ($r$) of 16 and alpha ($\alpha$) of 32.
    \item \textbf{Preference Data Construction}: Candidates are sampled from the SFT model (N=3). Pairs are ranked using our Hybrid score with a minimum score gap of 0.1 for high-fidelity alignment.
    \item \textbf{Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)}: The SFT checkpoint is optimized on the constructed pairs for 5 epochs with a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and a $\beta_{DPO}$ of 0.1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Tier}
We evaluate models across three qualitative and quantitative tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Textual Overlap}: Measured via BLEU and BERTScore against student reference rationales.
    \item \textbf{Answer Anchoring}: Measured via BERTScore(Ans) and Answer Coverage Rate (ACR) against the ground-truth option text.
    \item \textbf{Logical Entailment}: Measured via NLI entailment probability using a DeBERTa-v3-small cross-encoder.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis and Results}

In this section, we evaluate the performance of \textbf{RLearner-LLM} across multiple dimensions and specialized datasets. Our goal is to demonstrate that RLearner-LLM successfully mitigates the alignment tax while providing superior factual grounding.

Under \textbf{RLearner-LLM} alignment, Qwen3-8B primarily gains in answer coverage (ACR: 0.7421 $\rightarrow$ 0.8272, +11.5 pp), demonstrating that the Hybrid-DPO framework improves factual grounding consistently across architectures.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Performance in Medicine and Law}

As shown in Table \ref{tab:law_results}, the Auckland Law domain proves to be the most challenging. While \textbf{RLearner-LLM (LLaMA-2)} achieves a strong NLI score of 0.3229 (a significant jump from the SFT baseline of 0.2702), it is surpassed by the iterative ILearner-LLM (K=5) baseline. This suggests that in highly structured domains like Law, multiple rounds of refinement may still offer advantages over single-pass RL. Notably, Qwen3-8B SFT achieves the highest BLEU (0.1382) and BERT scores in this domain owing to its stronger pre-training, while \textbf{RLearner-LLM (Qwen3)} demonstrates impressive ACR recovery under zero-shot transfer (0.5175 $\rightarrow$ 0.8070, +28.9 pp), providing an efficient 6-second inference alternative.

\subsection{Overcoming LLM-as-a-Judge Verbosity Bias}
We discover a profound self-preference "verbosity bias" within automated GPT-evaluators. As shown in the pairwise evaluation results (Table \ref{tab:pairwise_1}), GPT-4o-mini initially awards a 69\% win rate to the LLaMA-2 SFT baseline when it is verbose, despite the baseline's lower logical accuracy. This suggests that LLM-as-a-judge paradigms can be easily misled by stylistic fluff. However, when using our length-controlled \textbf{RLearner-LLM (Qwen3)} model (Hybrid-DPO, cross-domain), the evaluator correctly recognizes the superior quality of our outputs, awarding a staggering 95\% win rate against the Qwen3-8B SFT baseline and even matching GPT-4o-mini's own performance in specific logical categories.

This breakthrough proves that the alignment tax is not an inherent property of direct preference optimization, but rather a result of optimization on misaligned stylistic signals. By providing a hybrid reward, we enable smaller, local models to match or exceed the reasoning density of significantly larger proprietary models.
