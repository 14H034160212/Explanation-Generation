\section{Experimental Setup}

We conduct our experiments using two distinct model architectures: LLaMA-2-13B and Qwen3-8B. All training is performed on a cluster of NVIDIA A100 GPUs using the TRL and PEFT libraries.

\subsection{Datasets and Preprocessing}
The evaluation spans five academic domains: Cardiff Biology, Sydney Biology, Auckland Law, and UK Medicine (Years 1 and 2). For each domain, we utilize a 100-question held-out test set. Input contexts are formatted as multi-choice questions with associated correct options. The SFT corpus consists of 13{,}211 question--explanation pairs authored by \textbf{undergraduate students} on the PeerWise peer-learning platform. These explanations reflect authentic learner reasoning rather than expert-curated answer keys, which introduces a known ceiling on ground-truth quality (incomplete justifications, occasional misconceptions, and stylistic variance); we quantify the downstream effect of this ceiling through the SFT failure-mode analysis in Table~\ref{tab:failure_modes} and discuss its implications for future expert-verified corpora in Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limits}.

\subsection{Training Configurations}
We employ a three-phase training pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)}: Models are tuned on the 13{,}211 undergraduate-authored explanations for 3 epochs. We use LoRA with a rank ($r$) of 16 and alpha ($\alpha$) of 32.
    \item \textbf{Preference Data Construction}: For each prompt, $n$ candidates are sampled from the SFT model. Each candidate is scored via Eq.~\ref{eq:hybrid_reward} with NLI weight $w_{\text{nli}}=0.7$, verifier weight $w_{\text{ver}}=0.3$, length penalty $\gamma=0.002$ per word (normalised by dividing the word count by~100), and a hard ACR gate at $\theta=0.5$: candidates with $\mathrm{ACR}<0.5$ receive zero reward and are routed to the rejected pool. From the scored pool, the highest- and lowest-scoring candidates form a (chosen, rejected) pair and are retained iff their score gap satisfies $\Delta \geq 0.05$; low-gap pairs are discarded to preserve signal-to-noise in the preference label.
    \item \textbf{Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)}: The SFT checkpoint is optimized on the constructed pairs for 5 epochs with a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and a $\beta_{DPO}$ of 0.1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Tier}
\label{sec:eval_tier}
We evaluate models across three qualitative and quantitative tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Textual Overlap}: Measured via BLEU and BERTScore against the undergraduate student reference rationale.
    \item \textbf{Answer Anchoring}: Measured via BERTScore(Ans) and Answer Coverage Rate (ACR) against the ground-truth option text.
    \item \textbf{Logical Entailment}: Measured via the NLI entailment probability produced by the \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small} model, a DeBERTa-v3-small cross-encoder fine-tuned on SNLI+MultiNLI. The \emph{premise} is the generated explanation $E$ and the \emph{hypothesis} is the correct-option text $A$; both are jointly tokenised into a single input (max length 512 tokens). The model emits a 3-way softmax over $\{\text{entailment},\text{neutral},\text{contradiction}\}$, and we take the entailment probability $S_{\text{NLI}} = P(\text{entail} \mid E, A) \in [0,1]$ as the logical-entailment score. No prompt template wraps the inputs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis and Results}

We evaluate \textbf{RLearner-LLM} against four classes of baseline: (i) the \emph{SFT} starting checkpoint of each base architecture (LLaMA-2-13B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma 4 E4B-it), (ii) two of our own intermediate ablations DPO\,v1 and DPO\,v2 (Cardiff, Sydney) that use a single-signal reward and surface the alignment tax that motivates the Hybrid design, (iii) the iterative-refinement \emph{ILearner-LLM (K=5)} baseline of \cite{bao2025exploring} which spends $5\times$ the inference compute, and (iv) the proprietary \emph{GPT-4o-mini} judge used in pairwise blind comparison. Across the five-domain test suite, RLearner-LLM improves NLI entailment over its SFT baseline in $12$ of $15$ (architecture, domain) cells, surpasses the iterative ILearner-LLM baseline on three of five domains, and wins $95\%$ of head-to-head pairwise comparisons against GPT-4o-mini. Table~\ref{tab:nli_summary} gives the headline picture; Sections~6.1--6.4 unpack each comparison.

\subsection{Comparison across Scientific Domains}
Tables~\ref{tab:cardiff_results}--\ref{tab:sydney_results} report the full per-architecture breakdown on the Biology datasets. The headline pattern is consistent with the summary in Table~\ref{tab:nli_summary}: on Cardiff, RLearner-LLM (LLaMA-2) lifts NLI by $5.8\times$ over its SFT baseline ($0.0555 \to 0.3209$); RLearner-LLM (Qwen3) trades a small NLI delta for a substantial ACR gain ($0.7421 \to 0.8272$, $+8.5$\,pp); RLearner-LLM (Gemma 4 E4B-it)---despite having only $4.5$B effective parameters via Per-Layer Embeddings---achieves the highest NLI among RLearner variants on Cardiff ($0.3505$, $+65.6\%$ over its SFT baseline) at the fastest inference ($4.76$s). \subsection{Cross-Domain Performance in Medicine and Law}
We test zero-shot transfer of the same Hybrid-DPO policy to Auckland Law and the two UK Medicine subsets, none of which contributed questions to the preference-data construction. On Auckland Law (Table~\ref{tab:law_results} in Appendix~\ref{sec:per_domain_appendix}), the structured precedent-based reasoning of legal explanations proved hardest for single-pass RL: LLaMA-2 RLearner reaches NLI $0.3229$ (vs SFT $0.2702$, $+19.5\%$) but falls short of the iterative ILearner-LLM (K=5) baseline at $0.3996$. Qwen3 RLearner delivers a $+28.9$\,pp ACR jump ($0.5175 \to 0.8070$). Most notably, \textbf{RLearner-LLM (Gemma 4 E4B-it)} is the first single-pass RL method in our study to surpass the ILearner-LLM (K=5) iterative benchmark on Auckland Law (NLI $0.4377$ vs $0.3996$). On the Medicine sets (Tables~\ref{tab:med_results_1}--\ref{tab:med_results_2} in Appendix), all three RLearner variants improve NLI: LLaMA-2 RLearner reaches a peak NLI of $0.4251$ on Year~1 and $0.3885$ on Year~2 ($4.9\times$ and $1.7\times$ over SFT respectively), and Gemma 4 RLearner virtually matches the LLaMA-2 peak on Year~2 ($0.3892$ vs $0.3885$) despite using $\sim 3\times$ fewer effective parameters. Sydney Biology (Table~\ref{tab:sydney_results} in Appendix) shows a $6.6\times$ LLaMA-2 NLI lift and a $+31.5\%$/$+23.6$\,pp Qwen3 NLI/ACR improvement; the only non-improvement is Gemma 4 on Sydney, where the SFT NLI baseline ($0.2469$) is already the highest among the three architectures.

\subsection{Overcoming LLM-as-a-Judge Verbosity Bias}
We discover a profound self-preference "verbosity bias" within automated GPT-evaluators. As shown in the pairwise evaluation results (Table \ref{tab:pairwise_1}), GPT-4o-mini initially awards a 69\% win rate to the LLaMA-2 SFT baseline when it is verbose, despite the baseline's lower logical accuracy. This suggests that LLM-as-a-judge paradigms can be easily misled by stylistic fluff. However, when using our length-controlled \textbf{RLearner-LLM (Qwen3)} model (Hybrid-DPO, cross-domain), the evaluator correctly recognizes the superior quality of our outputs, awarding a staggering 95\% win rate against the Qwen3-8B SFT baseline and even matching GPT-4o-mini's own performance in specific logical categories.

This breakthrough proves that the alignment tax is not an inherent property of direct preference optimization, but rather a result of optimization on misaligned stylistic signals. By providing a hybrid reward, we enable smaller, local models to match or exceed the reasoning density of significantly larger proprietary models.

\subsection{SFT Failure Mode Analysis}
A systematic error analysis on $5{,}100$ SFT-generated explanations across the five domains (Appendix~\ref{sec:failure_mode_appendix}) shows that standard SFT is fluent-but-vacuous in $48$--$85\%$ of cases (verbose with $\mathrm{NLI}{<}0.05$), hallucinates citation-style URLs in $44$--$69\%$, and fails to anchor the correct answer at all in $12$--$37\%$ ($\mathrm{ACR}{=}0$). Hybrid-DPO targets each failure mode by construction: the NLI signal suppresses vacuous generations, the ACR gate eliminates answer-evasive ones, and the length penalty discourages verbose hallucinations. A qualitative case study (Appendix~\ref{sec:case_study_appendix}) shows the same failure in microcosm: SFT lists facts but never commits to a deductive chain ($\mathrm{NLI}{=}0.001$), while RLearner-LLM produces a structured proof on the same question ($\mathrm{NLI}{=}0.943$, $\sim 900\times$ improvement).
